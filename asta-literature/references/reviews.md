# Reviews and citation checks

Read the user's existing report and bibliography before updating them. Reuse earlier searches when their scope and dates still fit. For each material claim, keep the paper identity, version, source link, evidence location and what the source actually supports.

- **Metadata only** supports bibliographic facts. An author's abstract can support an attributed overview. A retrieved passage supports the statement visible in that passage. Label a full-text check only after inspecting the relevant original section or PDF through an available authorized source.
- **Synthesis** should connect claims across sources, with citations beside the statements they support. Describe conflicting results in terms of differences in assumptions, populations or experimental settings. State what is still uncertain. Provide a small comparison table when it helps answer the question.
- **Mathematics example.** To check whether a later paper relaxes a finite-variance assumption, identify both results and inspect the theorem and assumptions in the versions cited. Compare the range of the moment parameter, independence assumptions, norm, probability statement and convergence rate. A passage mentioning “heavy tails” is a lead. If the theorem is unavailable, mark its assumptions for full-text verification.
- **Bibliography.** Resolve records by DOI, arXiv ID or paper ID. Verify authors, title, year and publication venue for the version cited. Reuse an existing citation key for that work, preserve project conventions and distinguish arXiv posting year from journal publication year. Leave unavailable bibliographic fields out. When asked to write files, add only the needed entries and check that every new citation resolves with the project's usual renderer or build.
